A LITTLE CONTEXT GOES A LONG WAY

Know what goes into your pace.

01 /

A transparent starting point

The baseline adds 0.25% to your pace for each °F above 55°F, up to 20%. It’s a product heuristic for planning, not a validated physiological model. The range is a planning band, not a confidence interval.

02 /

The weather when you ran

Strava runs are matched to estimated hourly weather at their starting location and time. Missing weather stays missing. Recent days use weather-model data; older runs use historical reanalysis.

03 /

History, with perspective

Personal trends compare flatter, steady 3–16 km runs at similar heart rates. With enough cool and hot runs, you can opt into a bounded blend of your observed trend and the baseline. It remains an experiment.

Comparison rules, estimate limits & privacy +

Comparable runs must have heart-rate and weather data, no race or workout tag, no indoor flag, no more than 15 m of ascent per km, and elapsed time within 25% of moving time. Heart rates must be within 10 bpm of the cohort median. Personalization needs 12 comparable runs, including 3 at or below 60°F and 3 at or above 75°F, plus a positive trend with R² ≥ 0.15.

The observed percentage slope is bounded to 0.1–0.6% per °F and blended with the baseline at a weight of run count / 60, capped at 50%. Humidity changes the conditions label, not the pace calculation. Fitness changes, fatigue, wind, sunshine, terrain, and training intent remain confounders.

Strava tokens stay on the server in temporary memory. Disconnect clears your local session; you can revoke app access in Strava settings. Run locations, rounded to approximately 1 km, and dates are sent to Open-Meteo to retrieve weather. They aren’t returned to the browser. Sessions are cleared when the server restarts. Strava access includes activities visible to everyone or followers; private activities aren’t requested.
